## Purpose of scheme categorisation
SEBI circular SEBI/HO/IMD/DF3/CIR/P/2017/114 dated 6 October 2017 requires every open-ended mutual fund scheme to be classified into one of five groups: Equity Schemes, Debt Schemes, Hybrid Schemes, Solution Oriented Schemes and Other Schemes. Each asset management company may offer only one scheme per category (with limited exceptions such as index funds, fund of funds and sectoral/thematic funds). The circular defines large cap, mid cap and small cap companies by full market capitalisation rank: large cap are the 1st to 100th companies, mid cap the 101st to 250th, and small cap the 251st company onwards.

## Large Cap Fund
An open-ended equity scheme predominantly investing in large cap stocks. The scheme must keep a minimum of 80% of total assets in equity and equity related instruments of large cap companies (top 100 companies by full market capitalisation).

## Mid Cap Fund
An open-ended equity scheme predominantly investing in mid cap stocks. The scheme must keep a minimum of 65% of total assets in equity and equity related instruments of mid cap companies (101st to 250th company by full market capitalisation).

## Small Cap Fund
An open-ended equity scheme predominantly investing in small cap stocks. The scheme must keep a minimum of 65% of total assets in equity and equity related instruments of small cap companies (251st company onwards by full market capitalisation). Small cap stocks are generally less liquid and more volatile than large cap stocks.

## Flexi Cap Fund
Introduced by SEBI circular SEBI/HO/IMD/DF3/CIR/P/2020/228 dated 6 November 2020. An open-ended dynamic equity scheme investing across large cap, mid cap and small cap stocks with a minimum of 65% of total assets in equity and equity related instruments. The fund manager has discretion over the market capitalisation mix.

## Contra Fund
An open-ended equity scheme following a contrarian investment strategy, with a minimum of 65% of total assets in equity and equity related instruments. An AMC may offer either a Value Fund or a Contra Fund, but not both.

## Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS)
An open-ended equity linked saving scheme with a statutory lock-in of 3 years and a tax benefit under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act. A minimum of 80% of total assets must be invested in equity and equity related instruments in accordance with the Equity Linked Saving Scheme, 2005 notified by the Ministry of Finance. Units cannot be redeemed before the 3-year lock-in ends.

## Sectoral and Thematic Fund
An open-ended equity scheme investing in a particular sector or following a particular theme, with a minimum of 80% of total assets in equity and equity related instruments of that sector or theme. Concentration in a single sector increases risk relative to diversified equity funds.

## Index Fund and Exchange Traded Fund
An open-ended scheme replicating or tracking a specific index, with a minimum of 95% of total assets in securities of the index being tracked. Index funds are passively managed; returns are expected to follow the index subject to tracking error and expenses.

## Liquid Fund
An open-ended liquid scheme investing in debt and money market securities with maturity of up to 91 days only. Liquid funds are designed for very short holding periods and carry low interest rate risk.

## Short Duration Fund
An open-ended short term debt scheme investing in instruments such that the Macaulay duration of the portfolio is between 1 year and 3 years.

## Corporate Bond Fund
An open-ended debt scheme predominantly investing in AA+ and above rated corporate bonds, with a minimum of 80% of total assets in such instruments.

## Gilt Fund
An open-ended debt scheme investing in government securities across maturities, with a minimum of 80% of total assets in government securities. Gilt funds carry no credit risk from the issuer but can be sensitive to interest rate movements.

## Conservative Hybrid Fund
An open-ended hybrid scheme investing predominantly in debt instruments: 10% to 25% of total assets in equity and equity related instruments and 75% to 90% of total assets in debt instruments.

## Dynamic Asset Allocation or Balanced Advantage Fund
An open-ended dynamic asset allocation fund that manages its investment in equity and debt dynamically. The equity exposure can change materially over time based on the fund's asset allocation model.

## Direct and Regular plans
Under SEBI circular CIR/IMD/DF/21/2012 dated 13 September 2012, every scheme must offer a separate Direct Plan for investors who purchase directly from the fund house without a distributor. Direct Plans have a lower expense ratio because no distribution commission is paid, and therefore a separate (usually higher) NAV than the Regular Plan of the same scheme.

## Total Expense Ratio limits
SEBI circular SEBI/HO/IMD/DF2/CIR/P/2018/137 dated 18 September 2018 introduced slab-based limits on the Total Expense Ratio (TER) effective 1 April 2019. For open-ended equity oriented schemes the maximum TER is 2.25% on the first Rs 500 crore of daily net assets, and for other open-ended schemes it is 2.00%, with lower limits for higher asset slabs. The TER is disclosed daily on AMC and AMFI websites.
